# Finance

## Money and the financial system

Money is a communication tool or language used for exchanging value. It evolved from barter (vymenny obchod), a system of mutual exchange where goods were traded directly, but it had limitations, such as the difficulty of matching needs between parties.

Early civilizations independently developed different forms of money (e.g., shells, salt, metal coins).

Over time, precious metals like gold and silver became the standard due to their durability and value.

The first paper money was introduced as a claim on stored gold.

### Three main functions of money

**Medium of Exchange** – Facilitates trade without needing a direct barter.

**Unit of Account** – Helps measure and compare the value of goods/services.

**Store of Value** – Allows wealth to be preserved and used later.

After WWII, the Bretton Woods Agreement tied global currencies to the US dollar, which was backed by gold.

In 1971, the US abandoned the Gold Standard (Nixon Shock), making money purely based on trust in the state - called **fiat money**

**Fiat money** is not backed by a physical commodity such as gold or silver. Their value is based on people's trust in the state and the central bank that issues them

Governments and central banks can print money without direct backing, leading to inflation.

## Inflation

**Inflation** is an increase in the general level of prices of goods and services in the economy, which is to blame for the production of new money by the government, which reduces the purchasing power of money, or - for the same money, we buy less

The 2008 financial crisis exposed weaknesses in the system, raising questions about fiat money's stability.

Fiat money suffers from constant inflation because central banks can print new money, reducing its purchasing power. Every year, the value of cash savings falls, forcing people to invest in assets such as stocks, real estate, cyrptocurrency or gold to preserve or increase the value of their wealth.

Taxes are mandatory payments individuals and businesses make to the government to fund public services, infrastructure, and social programs. They come in various forms, such as income tax, sales tax, and property tax, and help support the functioning of a country's economy and government.

**HDP (hrubý domácí produkt):** The total value of all goods and services produced in a given country over a period of time.

### 1. Basic Concept of Economics and Its Function

Economics is the study of how societies manage scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them among individuals. It's divided into two main branches:

**Microeconomics:** Deals with individual markets, households, and firms.

**Macroeconomics:** Deals with the overall economy, including growth, inflation, unemployment, and national policies.

**Supply and Demand:** At the core of economics is the principle of supply and demand, where prices are determined by how much of a good or service is available (supply) versus how much people want it (demand).

2\. Role of Banks in the Economy

Banks are critical to the functioning of an economy for several reasons:

Lending and Borrowing: Banks lend money to individuals, businesses, and governments. This allows economic activities such as starting new businesses, purchasing homes, and funding public projects. By controlling the interest rates, they influence the amount of borrowing and investment.

Money Creation: Through the fractional reserve banking system, banks create money. They only need to keep a small percentage of deposits in reserve, and can lend out the rest, thus increasing the money supply in the economy.

Interest Rates: Banks help determine the cost of borrowing by setting interest rates. Central banks, such as the Federal Reserve in the U.S., influence these rates by setting the federal funds rate, which in turn impacts the general economy.

Banking as a Regulatory Tool: Banks help regulate economic activity by controlling the flow of money. They can tighten or loosen credit depending on the economic situation, which can either stimulate or slow down growth.

3\. Monetary Policy: Central Banks and Their Role

Central Banks (e.g., Federal Reserve, European Central Bank) are responsible for managing the country's monetary policy. They regulate the money supply, interest rates, and credit availability to influence inflation, employment, and economic growth.

Tools of Monetary Policy:

Open Market Operations: Buying and selling government securities to increase or decrease the money supply.

Discount Rate: The interest rate at which banks can borrow from the central bank. Lowering it makes borrowing cheaper, increasing the money supply.

Reserve Requirements: Central banks can set the minimum amount of reserves that commercial banks must hold, which impacts how much they can lend out.

Inflation Control: Central banks work to control inflation by adjusting interest rates. When inflation is high, they might raise interest rates to slow down borrowing and spending. Conversely, if inflation is low and the economy is sluggish, they might lower interest rates to stimulate growth.

Quantitative Easing (QE): In times of economic crisis, central banks can inject money directly into the economy through large-scale asset purchases to lower interest rates and increase liquidity.

4\. Bank Regulation and Stability

Banks are heavily regulated to maintain financial stability and protect the economy from risk. Here’s how:

Capital Requirements: Banks are required to hold a certain amount of capital (reserve) relative to their loans to ensure they can absorb losses.

Deposit Insurance: To maintain public confidence, many countries have systems like the FDIC (in the U.S.), which insures deposits up to a certain limit to prevent bank runs.

Stress Tests: Banks undergo stress tests to ensure they can survive economic downturns or financial crises.

5\. Banks as Economic Stabilizers

Banks, along with central banks, help stabilize the economy. By controlling credit supply, they can dampen or amplify economic cycles. For example, in a recession, central banks can lower interest rates and banks can increase lending to stimulate spending and investment.

Banks also help smooth out the flow of goods and services in the economy by providing financing to different sectors, ensuring that businesses can continue operations even during downturns.

6\. Banks in Global Economics

In the context of global economics, banks play a key role in facilitating international trade by offering foreign exchange services and facilitating cross-border lending.

Global Central Banks coordinate monetary policies to avoid instability in international markets. They influence global supply chains and investment flows.

7\. The Importance of Sound Banking Systems

A sound banking system is essential for economic growth. Well-functioning banks ensure that:

Savings are safely stored and efficiently allocated for investment.

Credit is accessible to consumers and businesses, facilitating economic activity.

The economy can respond to changes in demand, technology, and other factors.

Poorly regulated or unstable banks can lead to crises. For example, the 2008 financial crisis was largely caused by the collapse of banks that had not adequately managed risk, leading to widespread economic downturns.

8\. The Interplay Between Fiscal and Monetary Policy

Fiscal policy (government spending and taxation) and monetary policy (central bank actions) work together to regulate the economy. Central banks, by adjusting interest rates and controlling money supply, directly affect how much the government can spend and how much citizens and businesses can borrow.

Coordination between governments and central banks is key in preventing or managing economic crises.

9\. Challenges and Limitations of Banks

Banks cannot fully control the economy. Factors like political stability, technological changes, global markets, and natural disasters can all influence economic conditions.

Over-regulation or under-regulation can both have detrimental effects on the economy. A balance must be struck between free market forces and necessary oversight.

Moral hazard can also be a problem, where banks take excessive risks because they know they’ll be bailed out by the government in case of failure.

Net earnings per share are the net earnings of a company (i.e., earnings after deducting all costs, taxes, etc.) divided by the number of all shares issued. This indicator indicates how much profit a company generated for every single share issued.

Conversely, a low P/E ratio may indicate that the stock is relatively cheap compared to its earnings potential.

### Bitcoin

Bitcoin was created in 2009 by an anonymous entity known as Satoshi Nakamoto. It was designed primarily as a decentralized, digital store of value and a peer-to-peer payment system. No intermediaries like banks are involved—just sender and receiver.

Bitcoin has a fixed supply of 21 million coins, making it deflationary and resistant to inflation. It operates on a proof-of-work (PoW) consensus mechanism, which requires miners to solve complex mathematical puzzles to validate transactions and secure the network. This makes Bitcoin highly secure but relatively slow, with a transaction processing time of about 10 minutes per block.

Bitcoin has value mainly as a store of value, rather than as a conventional currency. While Bitcoin's fixed supply limits inflation, it lacks the flexibility to stabilize the economy in times of crisis, something that centralized systems can achieve.

Bitcoins are not physical or stored anywhere.

What you own is access to a key pair (public & private keys) associated with recorded transactions on the blockchain.

**Blockchain** is a decentralized digital ledger that records transactions across a network of computers in a secure, immutable, and transparent manner. It ensures trust without the need for intermediaries by using consensus mechanisms like Proof of Work or Proof of Stake.

**Public Key:** Like an email address—safe to share. (or a hashed version of it) serves as the wallet address (like a bank account number).

**Private Key:** Like a password—must be kept secret. is the proof of ownership—used to sign transactions and spend coins.

These keys are generated using cryptographic algorithms (e.g., Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm).

You can derive a public key from a private key, but not the other way around.

Having bitcoins means having the private key to spend BTC sent to your public address.

Anyone can create unlimited wallets locally and offline, without registration.

Wallets remain invisible to the blockchain until they receive coins.

The blockchain only tracks transactions, not a full list of existing wallets.

It’s possible to send coins to any address, even if the wallet doesn't exist (no address validity check).

If an invalid address is used, the coins are lost forever—unless the private key can be guessed (which is practically impossible).

You can transfer funds to a wallet even if it's never been online.

#### Transaction workflow

The sender signs the transaction using their private key.

The transaction is broadcast to the network and verified by miners.

Miners confirm ownership and validate the transaction (called mining).

**Role of Mining:**

Adds transactions to the blockchain.

Introduces new bitcoins as block rewards.

### Blockchain consensus mechanisms

In the world of blockchain and cryptocurrencies, consensus mechanisms are protocols that help distributed networks agree on the state of the blockchain. These mechanisms ensure security, decentralization, and efficiency. Here’s a breakdown of Proof of Stake (PoS) and some other major consensus mechanisms:

1. **Proof of Stake (PoS)**

PoS is an alternative to Proof of Work (PoW) that aims to be more energy-efficient. Instead of miners competing to solve cryptographic puzzles, validators (stakers) are chosen to create new blocks and confirm transactions based on the amount of cryptocurrency they hold and are willing to “stake” as collateral.

How it works:

• Validators lock up a certain amount of their cryptocurrency as a stake.

• The network randomly selects a validator to confirm the next block, usually weighted by the amount staked.

• Validators earn rewards (transaction fees or new coins) for their work but can lose part of their stake if they act maliciously (slashing).

Pros:

✅ Energy-efficient compared to PoW

✅ Faster transactions and scalability

✅ Less hardware demand

Cons:

❌ Wealthier participants have more influence (as they can stake more)

❌ Possible centralization risks

Examples: Ethereum 2.0, Cardano (ADA), Solana (SOL), Polkadot (DOT)

2. **Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS)**

A variation of PoS where token holders vote for a small number of delegates (validators) who validate transactions on their behalf.

Pros:

✅ Faster transactions and lower fees

✅ More democratic (token holders vote for validators)

Cons:

❌ Can lead to centralization (since only a few delegates control the network)

Examples: EOS, TRON, Binance Smart Chain

3. **Proof of Work (PoW)**

The original consensus mechanism used by Bitcoin and Ethereum before its transition to PoS. Miners solve complex mathematical puzzles to validate transactions and secure the network.

Pros:

✅ Highly secure

✅ Decentralized (if mining power is distributed)

Cons:

❌ High energy consumption

❌ Slower transactions compared to PoS

Examples: Bitcoin (BTC), Litecoin (LTC), Dogecoin (DOGE)

4. **Proof of Authority (PoA)**

A consensus model where a limited number of approved validators (often organizations or known entities) validate transactions.

Pros:

✅ Extremely fast and scalable

✅ Low energy consumption

Cons:

❌ Centralized (trust is placed in a few validators)

Examples: VeChain, some private blockchains

5. **Proof of Burn (PoB)**

Users “burn” (permanently destroy) a portion of their cryptocurrency to gain the right to validate transactions and create blocks.

Pros

✅ Reduces inflation by removing coins from circulation

✅ Incentivizes long-term commitment

Cons:

❌ Wastes financial resources (coins are lost permanently)

Examples: Counterparty (XCP), Slimcoin

6. **Proof of Space & Proof of Time (PoST)**

Used by the Chia (XCH) blockchain, this system relies on users allocating storage space rather than computing power.

Pros:

✅ Lower energy consumption compared to PoW

✅ Uses hard drive space instead of GPUs/ASICs

Cons:

❌ Requires large amounts of storage

❌ Hardware wear and tear over time

What happens when all the bitcoins are mined?

1. Block reward – e.g. now (after the April 2024 halving) it is 3.125 BTC per block.

**The Lightning Network (LN)** is a so-called Layer 2 on top of Bitcoin, which allows for extremely fast and cheap transactions without having to write each one directly to the blockchain.

**How the Lightning Network works:**

1. Open a payment channel with another user – this will create a transaction on the main Bitcoin blockchain.
2. Within this channel, you can send bitcoins back and forth with the other party instantly and with little or no fee.
3. As soon as you don't want to send anymore, close the channel – the resulting balance (how much belongs to whom) is recorded in the blockchain.

Think of it as opening a backup account where you and a friend send money back and forth, but you only go to the bank once — at the beginning and at the end.

**Advantages of the Lightning Network:**

• Speed – transactions happen in milliseconds.

• Low fees – ideal for microtransactions (e.g. paying for coffee, online content, etc.).

• Scalability – Bitcoin can handle millions of transactions per second outside the main blockchain thanks to LN.

**Application in practice:**

• El Salvador uses the Lightning Network for regular Bitcoin payments.

• Wallets such as Wallet of Satoshi, Phoenix, or BlueWallet already support Lightning.

Many companies accept Bitcoin through LN because the fees are a fraction of a cent.

### Custody and wallets (owning crypto)

When you keep your Bitcoin on an exchange, you don’t actually own the private keys—the exchange does

**Not Your Keys, Not Your Coins** – If the exchange gets hacked, goes bankrupt, or freezes withdrawals, you could lose access to your Bitcoin.

You Rely on the Exchange's Security – Your funds are only as safe as the exchange’s security measures.

Limited Control – The exchange can restrict withdrawals, impose limits, or even block your account.

Move it to a non-custodial wallet (e.g., hardware wallet, software wallet) where you control the private keys

Remember your Bitcoin is on the blockchain, the seed phrase is your private key that gives you the access to move it

#### Hardware wallets (Tangem, Ledger, Trezor)

**Cold hardware wallet** store private keys offline (more secure), whereas **hot hardware wallet** uses internet to setup the wallet, which is less secure

Can’t be accessed remotely by hackers.

Require a PIN + seed phrase for recovery.

**Seed phrase**, also known as a seed recovery phrase, backup seed phrase, or mnemonic phrase, is a sequence of random words that stores the data required to access or recover cryptocurrency on blockchains or crypto wallets. This phrase is generated by the wallet software and is encouraged to be recorded on paper for safekeeping. This is particularly useful in scenarios such as hardware breakdowns or file corruption, where the user can reinstall the required wallet software and regain access to their assets via their paper.

Participating in the cryptocurrency space comes with great responsibility. A seed phrase needs to be kept a secret at all costs, as anyone who knows a seed phrase can import a copy of a wallet onto their own device and empty it. This is due to the fact that knowledge of a seed phrase can potentially grant access to private keys. Therefore, it's essential to keep a seed phrase safe and private to protect digital assets. [https://www.coinbase.com/th/learn/wallet/what-is-a-seed-phrase](https://www.coinbase.com/th/learn/wallet/what-is-a-seed-phrase)

#### Non-custodial software wallets (MetaMask, Trust Wallet, Electrum)

Store private keys on your device or in the cloud of the provider (riskier than hardware wallets)

Require a seed phrase for backup.

Vulnerable to malware, phishing, or hacks.

**1.**

**Půjčky kryté Bitcoinem (tzv. crypto-backed loans)**

Když někdo „půjčuje proti Bitcoinu“, znamená to, že Bitcoin dá jako zástavu – podobně jako když ručíš domem u hypotéky.

* Platformy jako Ledn, Strike nebo Coinbase to skutečně nabízejí.
* Nemusíš Bitcoin prodávat, jen ho „zastavíš“ a dostaneš půjčku v dolarech nebo stablecoinech.
* U některých půjček nemusíš měsíčně splácet – úrok se jen přičítá k dluhu a vše se splatí až na konci nebo při výběru zástavy.

Ale pozor:

* Když cena Bitcoinu klesne, může přijít tzv. margin call – musíš doplnit více Bitcoinu, nebo ti platforma část Bitcoinu prodá (likvidace).
* Když cena Bitcoinu stoupne, můžeš půjčku „přetočit“ (refinancovat) nebo si půjčit víc.
* Je to riziková strategie, hlavně při velké volatilitě trhu.

**2.**

**Půjčky přes brokerský účet (klasické finance)**

Komentář od hannashanar popisoval margin půjčku – to je, když si půjčíš proti svému portfoliu akcií nebo fondů:

* Banka nebo broker ti půjčí peníze s nízkým úrokem (např. 4–6 %).
* Většinou nemusíš měsíčně splácet – úrok se jen přičítá k dluhu.
* Pokud tvoje portfolio roste rychleji než úrok, můžeš půjčku držet dlouhodobě a portfolio dál zhodnocovat.
* Bohatí lidé to často používají, aby měli peníze bez nutnosti prodeje akcií (tím se vyhnou daním z kapitálového zisku).

Ale:

* Když hodnota portfolia klesne, opět hrozí margin call – musíš doplnit hotovost nebo část portfolia prodají.
* Pokud úrok převýší výnosy, dluh ti roste.
* Není to „zadarmo“ – jen se splátky odsouvají v čase.

**3.**

**„Žádné měsíční splátky“ – polopravda**

Ano, může být pravda, že nemusíš měsíčně nic platit, ale:

* Úrok se dál přičítá k jistině.
* Dluh tedy roste, dokud ho nakonec nezaplatíš nebo se neprodá zástava.

**4.**

**Shrnutí**

* Tyto půjčky existují a fungují, jak v kryptu, tak v klasickém investování.
* Princip: půjčuješ si proti aktivu, které doufáš, že poroste.
* Funguje to jen, dokud hodnota aktiva roste. Pokud padne, můžeš o něj přijít.
* Je to běžný nástroj pro bohaté investory, ale pro běžného člověka velmi riziková strategie.
